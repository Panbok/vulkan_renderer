description: Apply to all written responses and edited prose. Remove filler and ambiguity while preserving technical meaning.
---

# Unslop

State the result or required action first. Use concrete nouns, active verbs
and one term per concept. Keep the user's tone and the distinctions needed to
understand the result.

- Replace praise, slogans and claims such as "robust" or "efficient" with the
  mechanism, constraint or measured result. Keep an established project motto
  when the surrounding rules define what it requires.
- Cut filler, promotional adjectives, generic conclusions, invented metaphors
  and repeated summaries. Do not add opinions, anecdotes or deliberate disorder
  to make technical writing seem human.
- Turn vague instructions into a trigger, action and observable completion
  condition. State who owns a value and when it changes.
- Prefer short connected paragraphs. Use lists for parallel requirements and
  tables for comparisons. Use sentence-case headings and restrained emphasis.
- Remove canned contrast such as "not just X, but Y". State the required behavior
  directly. Preserve a contrast when it expresses a necessary technical boundary.
- Preserve code, identifiers, quotations, units, uncertainty and evidence.
  Punctuation and technical vocabulary are tools, not words to ban by pattern.

Before sending or saving, remove any sentence that repeats another sentence or
adds no action, fact, constraint or reason. Check that shortening did not erase
an exception, lifetime rule or acceptance condition.
