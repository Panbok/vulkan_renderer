acceptance thresholds. Moving-camera differences cannot prove flicker reduction.

Remaining sparse-clamp finding (policy unchanged):
With current RGB 10, accepted history RGB 2, equal coverage and weight 0.85,
a single supported current ray creates bounds [10,10]. Production filtering
returns 10, while ordinary accumulation would return 3.2. Thus history cannot
smooth RGB in a single-hit neighborhood even when metadata accepts it.
The 19,968-pixel under-bar ROI contains 380/367/411 active single-hit neighborhoods
at frames 3/4/5. Their output RGB matches the lone current ray within one FP16 ULP.
Pixels with a single-hit neighborhood at either transition endpoint account for
12.08%/12.65% of covered-RGB variation; one-or-two-hit endpoints account for
22.24%/22.97%. Previously active pixels becoming empty account for 4.93%/5.11%.
Captures do not establish whether those previous samples pass reprojected metadata.
Relaxing this clamp needs a separate quality decision because receiver metadata
cannot reject reflected-object motion; it trades shimmer against reflection lag.
No such relaxation has been implemented in this change.
